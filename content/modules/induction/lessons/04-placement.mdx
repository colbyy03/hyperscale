## How this works

There are three questions in each area. A rough sum on paper is fine; looking things up isn't, because the point is to measure what's already in your head. Only your first answer counts. When you're done, the summary at the bottom says, for each area, whether to move briskly, take it at a normal pace, or start from the basics.

Two reasons this matters:

- **Time goes where it's needed.** Re-teaching what you already know wastes your hours, and skipping what you don't know costs you later. Guided worked examples help beginners most and can actively slow down people who already know the material, so the course adapts how much scaffolding it gives.
- **Test-outs.** Where your MDS courses already cover a module well (probability is the clearest case), its checkpoint is open from day one. A strong result here tells you to try it.
